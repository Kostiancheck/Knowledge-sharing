# Immersion, Addiction, Obsession

## Immersion

Dota is extremely immersive. Once creeps meet in lane, you can't really think about anything other than dota. There is just an overwhelming amount of information to process, especially considering all the hidden information and the element of randomness.

It is very easy to achieve something like a flow state in Dota. And maybe more importantly, you can achieve it consistently. I can't come up with a single other activity that would put me in the flow state in 99/100 cases.

Anything immersive enough is a great tool for escapism, but Dota is exceptionally effective at it. It is designed to be.

1. Variety - 127 heroes, hundreds of items.
2. Timings - there is always 'the next step' in terms of timings. Something to look up to.
3. Game duration - 45 minutes target is basically the longest it can be without being physically exhaustive and/or logistically inconvenient.
4. Social aspect - you might get away with not talking, but you definitely must communicate in _some_ way.
5. Patches - Dota averages like 2 major patches a year, so you don't have enough time to 'solve' it.

## Addiction

Immersion is what happens within a single match. Just like anything immersive enough, it can potentially turn into addiction.

> Note that I use the term 'immersion'. Dota is immersive rather than pleasurable, and I think everybody who has played a couple of Dota matches knows why.

Some people who want to go deep into escapism, might seek out this flow state over and over again. This can lead to addiction. There are a few mechanisms in place that enable this behavior.

1. Rating
2. Guilds
3. Hero mastery levels

I personally don't see any issue with it, because addiction is a spectrum. I believe light addiction might even be beneficial in certain situations, and it is obviously better to be addicted to Dota rather than to drugs, smoking, or anime.

But there definitely is a line where addiction becomes harmful. If you skip school or work to play Dota, this is a bad sign. If you neglect your health, friendships, family or love interests to play Dota, this is definitely a sign of heavy addiction.

However, it is also important to know _why_ you are addicted to Dota.

## Why

It is probably an oversimplification to say that you have one primary reason to play Dota, but for the sake of the argument, let's suppose it's true.

Why would you want to play Dota a lot?

__You want to be The Best__

This is a bad reason to play Dota. 

First of all, Dota is a team game. Every win is a team effort. Even within your specific role or niche or current patch, it is impossible to select one undisputed 'Best' player. 

Second, even if communication technologies reach maximum efficiency, you will still be limited by the speed of light, so there will still be some regionality involved. In a game where milliseconds matter, it is impossible to fairly set up a match between Europe and Australia. The closest thing we can get is The International, and it is a single tournament where single hiccup can end your run.

Third, what are you going to do if you do achieve your goal? [Post-Olympic blues](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/5650965/2024/08/11/post-olympic-blues-medal-depression/) is a real thing, and it happens to Dota players post-TI too. Reaching a pinncale of your career feels great for a moment (I imagine), but it also means it is downhill from there.

__You want to escape reality__

I guess this a legitimate reason to play Dota, and as I mentioned earlier, Dota is one of the best tools of escapism. However, as it is generally the case with escapism, you should not treat it as a long-term solution, and you should definitely address your underlying problems if possible.

__You want to hit a certain MMR, or certain hero level__

[MMR is just a number](https://dota2voicelines.com/#:~:text=MMR%20is%20just%20a%20number). It won't give you neither satisfaction, nor street credibility.

__You just really like the game__

Now we're talking!

## Obsession

Obsession is very different from addiction. There are several ways to define it; I will stick to the following distinction:

> A guy collecting whiskey is obsessed, a guy drinking yorsh under the bridge is addicted.

Technically, both can be alcoholics, so this is kind of a bad example, but you get my point.

It is definitely possible to become obsessed with Dota. In fact, I think most TI winners were obsessed with Dota, rather than actually actively trying to win TI.

In fact, I will make an argument that __obsession is the best way to live__.

### Religion

I think every seriously religious person is obsessed.

> A Providence watches over each man’s wandering through life. It provides him with two guides. The one calls him forward. The other calls him back. They are, however, not in opposition to each other, these two guides, nor do they leave the wanderer standing there in doubt, confused by the double call. Rather the two are in eternal understanding with each other. For the one beckons forward to the Good, the other calls man back from evil. Nor are they blind guides. Just for that reason there are two of them. For in order to make the journey secure, they must look both forward and backward.

At any age you are at the equal distance from death, from the two guides. When you are just born, a mere year ago you were dead. When you are about to cast your last breath, you will be dead soon.

I think there is some beauty in the eternal struggle of Good and Evil in Dota.
